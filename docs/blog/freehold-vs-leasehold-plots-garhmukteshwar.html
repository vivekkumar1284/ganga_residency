Defining Freehold and Leasehold Land Tenure

Before making a purchase, every property buyer must understand how ownership rights differ legally:

  • Freehold Land Ownership: You own both the land plot and the construction above it in perpetuity (forever). You hold full, unconditional legal rights to transfer, sell, renovate, lease, or pass down the land to future generations without requiring government or authority permission.
  • Leasehold Land Ownership: The underlying land belongs to a state development authority, municipality, or private lessor. You buy the right to occupy the property for a specified period (typically 30, 60, or 99 years). Upon lease expiry, you must pay renewal fees or face land reversion.

The Hidden Risks of Leasehold Properties

While leasehold properties may occasionally feature lower entry prices, they carry hidden long-term liabilities:

  • Lease Expiry Uncertainty: Once the lease tenure expires, land ownership reverts to the original lessor unless a renewal fee is paid.
  • Transfer Fee Demands: Selling leasehold land often involves paying transfer charges (often 1% to 5% of market value) to state development boards.
  • Strict Modification Limits: Structural alterations or subdivision of plot sizes require explicit authority permissions.
