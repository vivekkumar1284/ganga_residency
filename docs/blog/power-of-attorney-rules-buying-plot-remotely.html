1. What is a Power of Attorney (POA) in Indian Real Estate?

A Power of Attorney is a legally binding legal instrument governed by the Powers of Attorney Act, 1882, and the Indian Contract Act, 1872. It permits a principal (the investor buying the plot) to authorize an agent (a trusted family member, lawyer, or legal representative) to act on their behalf for property selection, agreement execution, stamp duty payment, and sub-registrar office registration.

3. Legal Registration Rules and Stamp Duty in Uttar Pradesh

Under Section 17 of the Indian Registration Act (as amended for Uttar Pradesh), any Power of Attorney creating an interest in immovable property or authorizing an agent to execute sale deeds must be compulsorily registered. Unregistered POAs hold little to no legal validity in UP revenue courts.

Key compliance protocols mandated for registering a POA in Uttar Pradesh include:

  • Stamp Duty Payment: In UP, stamp duty on POAs varies depending on whether the attorney is a blood relative (father, mother, son, daughter, brother, sister, husband, wife) or a non-relative. Concessionary stamp duty rates apply strictly to blood relatives.
  • Notarization & Attestation: If the principal resides outside India, the POA must be attested by the Indian Embassy or Consulate in that country, followed by adjudication upon arrival in India.
  • Two Witnesses: The signing of the POA must be witnessed by two independent, credible individuals whose identity proofs (Aadhaar/PAN) are submitted alongside.
  • Photograph and Biometrics: Both the principal (if present) or authorized agent and witnesses must complete biometric verification at the local sub-registrar office.

5. Common Pitfalls to Avoid When Using a POA

While POAs offer remarkable convenience, failing to observe legal nuances can result in severe financial disputes. Avoid these common traps:

Pitfall 1: Using General Drafts from the Internet. Generic templates downloaded online frequently lack UP-specific revenue clauses, rendering them legally void during registrar scrutiny. Always use developer-vetted legal drafts tailored for Residential Plots in garhmukteshwar.

Pitfall 2: Failing to Revoke upon Completion. If you executed a GPA instead of an SPA, remember to formally revoke the document through a registered cancellation deed once your transaction at Ganga Residency is fully completed.

Pitfall 3: Appointing Unverified Agents. Always select an attorney whom you trust implicitly with financial instruments, and ensure bank transactions flow directly from your primary bank account to the developer's official escrow/company account.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) on POA Plot Buying

Can an NRI buy residential plots in Uttar Pradesh using a POA?

Yes. NRIs can purchase plots in UP through a registered Special Power of Attorney. Funds must originate from NRE/NRO accounts in compliance with RBI FEMA guidelines.

Is physical presence required at all for registry at Ganga Residency?

No. With a duly registered Special Power of Attorney and video verification protocols where applicable, your legal attorney can complete the entire sub-registrar registry on your behalf.

What happens if the principal passes away after issuing a POA?

Under Indian contract law, a Power of Attorney terminates immediately upon the death of the principal. Any transaction attempted thereafter using the POA becomes null and void.
