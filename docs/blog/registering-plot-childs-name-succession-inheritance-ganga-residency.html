1. The Legal Framework: Can a Minor Own Property in India?

Under Indian Contract Act, 1872, a minor (any individual under 18 years of age) is legally incompetent to enter into a binding contract, which traditionally prevents them from purchasing property directly in their own name. However, Indian property law does permit a minor to acquire property by way of gift, inheritance, or legal transfer, provided certain legal safeguards and fiduciary structures are established.

When purchasing a plot at Ganga Residency for your child, you generally have two primary pathways:

Natural Guardianship

Purchasing the plot in your own name as natural guardian or executing a registered Gift Deed post-purchase in favor of your minor child.

Direct Gift Deed

Transferring ownership legally through a registered gift deed where parents act as donors and the minor child acts as the recipient.

Major Age Transition

Structuring the asset so that full independent ownership and mutation rights vest automatically upon the child attaining 18 years of age.

2. Exploring Uttar Pradesh Land Revenue Codes & Minor Registration

In the state of Uttar Pradesh, land registry and mutation processes are governed by the Uttar Pradesh Revenue Code, 2006. When registering property for children within garhmukteshwar district jurisdictions, sub-registrar offices require absolute clarity regarding natural guardianship.

If the child is a minor, all sale agreements, stamp duty payments, and registry documents must explicitly state the name of the father or mother acting as the natural guardian and legal manager of the minor's estate. Once the child attains majority, a simple mutation (Dakhil Kharij) application is filed to transition the land records entirely into their independent name. 3. Financial and Tax Implications of Transferring Property to Children

Estate planning requires a meticulous understanding of income tax laws, clubbing of income provisions, and stamp duty rates in Uttar Pradesh. Making informed financial choices prevents unexpected tax liabilities later.

Review the core financial and tax parameters compared below:

Tax & Legal Parameter Transfer via Gift Deed (Parent to Minor Child) Direct Purchase in Adult Child's Name
Income Tax (Section 64) Any income generated from the gifted asset (e.g., rental or farm yield) is clubbed with the parent's income until the child attains majority. No clubbing of income applies; the adult child files independent tax returns on any generated income.
Stamp Duty in UP Gift deeds executed between parents and children enjoy concessional stamp duty rates and nominal registration fees in Uttar Pradesh. Standard residential plot stamp duty rates applicable across urban/semi-urban municipal jurisdictions in UP.
Capital Gains Liability When the child sells the property after becoming a major, the holding period includes the parents' holding duration for indexation benefits. Holding period calculated strictly from the date of initial purchase and registration in the adult child's name.
Revocability & Control A registered gift deed is absolute and irrevocable once executed; parents cannot reclaim the property unilaterally. Absolute ownership rests with the adult child from day one with zero parental revocation rights.
